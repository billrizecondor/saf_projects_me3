What would it take to fly the Philippines on e-kerosene?

Batteries are too heavy for most flights, so aviation needs drop-in liquid fuels. This study sizes the solar or wind capacity, land and investment needed to replace all Philippine jet fuel with e-kerosene. The fuel is made from CO₂ captured directly from the air (DAC) and hydrogen from renewable electricity.

The land footprint

The squares are drawn to scale by area. Wind turbines need wide spacing, so wind needs far more land than solar even though it needs fewer megawatts. Much of that land can still be farmed between turbines.

Key findings

€4.8–7.9 billion up frontis the 2030-cost CAPEX to switch all current jet fuel to e-kerosene, falling to €3.4–6.0 billion with 2050 technology prices.
28–42% more renewableson top of the country's 20 GW goal for 2040 would be needed to power fuel production alone.
Solar uses about 5% of wind's landbut needs 18–23 million panels. Land use has to be planned carefully either way.
Cost is the deciding factorPlant CAPEX drives the final fuel price, so falling DAC and renewable costs are critical for this pathway to work.
