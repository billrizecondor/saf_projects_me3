# Transitioning to Sustainable Aviation Fuels: The Philippines

Estimates the **energy, land and investment** needed to replace the Philippines' current jet fuel demand with sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). The pathway studied is **e-kerosene** made with CO₂ from **Direct Air Capture (DAC)** and powered by solar or wind energy.

Course: BME Energy Storage, 2025.

📄 Paper: [Energy Storage Paper (PDF)](<Energy Storage Paper - Condor.pdf>) · 📊 Model: [SAF Calculations.xlsx](<SAF Calculations.xlsx>)

## Why SAF?

Batteries have too little energy and power density for most aviation. SAF, such as HEFA, alcohol-to-jet and e-fuels, offers drop-in replacements for fossil jet fuel. Their emissions are near zero only if their production runs on renewable energy.

## Key findings

- The estimated investment is about **€7.9 billion** to move current Philippine jet fuel demand to SAF.
- The pathway depends on **technology costs falling** over the coming decades.
- The **land footprint** of the renewable plants needed is large and must be managed.
- The Philippines' abundant renewable resources make it a strong candidate for this pathway.
